Is this really happening?

It's early — and worth being straight with you about how early. People already ask ChatGPT to find them a gift, tell Alexa to reorder something, or let an app pick a brand for them. What's changing is the last step: assistants are starting to place the order themselves, without a person clicking Buy. That part is new, it is not yet common, and nobody can tell you exactly how fast it grows.

That creates a problem nobody had before. A person can look at a website and tell it's the real shop. Software can't. And a fake copy of a store is cheap to make. So a careful assistant does the same thing you'd do with a stranger asking for your card details — it plays it safe and skips the shop it can't confirm. Nothing to do with the quality of your store; it simply has no way to check.

This page is about giving it a way to say yes to you — before that matters, rather than after.

Step 1, in real life

This is the only technical bit on the whole page. It's the same kind of setting you (or whoever set up your site) used to make your email work. You paste two short lines into wherever your web address lives — Shopify, GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace — and that's the end of it.

What you paste in
TypeTXT
Name_shelfprotocol
Valueshelfprotocol-verify=(we give you this part)

If that means nothing to you, that is completely normal and you do not need to learn it. This is a job for whoever looks after your website — or, if that's nobody, for your website provider's support chat, who do it every day.

Not technical? Forward this

"Hi — can you add a TXT record to our domain for me? The name is _shelfprotocol and I'll send you the value. It's for a store verification thing. It shouldn't affect the website or email."

That's the entire request. Most providers do it while you wait.
